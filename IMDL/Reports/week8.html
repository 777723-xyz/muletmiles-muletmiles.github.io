Weekly Report 8 due March 8, 2016

 

 One Spring Break later and I finally think I'm starting to see the finish line! As a quick refresher of what happened before the break, my initial design (that was pictured in the last report) actually failed to move the robot base! After an incredibly underwhelming and frustrating back-up robot was created for object avoidance, I naturally set about finding what was wrong with my initial system.

        The problem, it turned out, was that the 6” omni-wheels that I were using were simply too heavy – the brushless motors could not generate enough torque. This was much to my dismay, as I had (of course) done preliminary calculations that filled me with the sense that the motors would have more than enough torque to overcome the robot's weight. Alas, this was not the case. To future readers out there: if your motor specifications do not include how much torque it can generate, be wary! The final number that you calculate might be a dramatic overestimation.

        To add to my woes, over Spring Break I attempted to make a second platform that utilized brushed motors (and, more importantly, transmissions) that I took off of hobbyist RC cars. Unfortunately, even with the transmissions, this still caused the same problem: the wheels were too heavy. You can imagine my frustration, especially since, as a Computer Engineer, I thought that simply getting a robot that could move would be the easy part.

        Naturally, I was presented with two options: go with smaller wheels or larger motors. I'm in the lucky position that I have connections to an entrepreneur who had access to more powerful motors and ESCs, and he was kind enough to let me 4 of each for the remainder of the semester. The result, finally, was that I could move the darn robot.

        The problem now, of course, is that the huge motors drain a significant amount of power. I decided to splurge and purchase a $70 SLA battery from Batteries+, which seems to do the job for now.

        Long story short, my original design (and backup design) have been completely thrown out. The new bot utilizes 4 CIM Motors (from Vex Robotics), 4 corresponding Victor SP speed controllers (also from Vex), and an 18Ah 12V SLA battery (from Duracell). As you can imagine, the robot is now likely on the larger/heavier end for the class!

        Looking ahead, I still haven't even begun to interface my special sensor with the robot (though I've written what I hope to be all the OpenCV code that I'll need), and the robot had to be completely taken apart to travel back to Gainesville. Long story short, I'll need a miracle to be ready for the Thursday Morning demo date. Here's hoping!
